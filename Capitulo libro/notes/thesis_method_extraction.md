## Research problem and objective

- The thesis formulates the central question as how sustainable tourism can be practiced on the Camino de Santiago in Spain during the COVID-19 pandemic.
- The main objective is to determine methods or forms of sustainable tourism on the Camino de Santiago in pandemic conditions.
- The thesis also states specific aims that include identifying the sustainable tourism process on the Camino, gathering stakeholder information through interviews, examining economic, social, and environmental impacts, and producing recommendations for managing contagion risks on the route.

## Methodological design explicitly stated in the thesis

- Approach: qualitative.
- Design: action research.
- Sampling: non-probabilistic, criterion-based selection.
- Data collection technique: interviews.
- Analytical support: literature review plus interview analysis.

The thesis explicitly says that the qualitative approach was chosen because it allows interpretation of the object of study in its natural context. It also states that action research was selected to respond to the problem, support decision-making, and improve concrete practices.

## Population, stakeholders, and sampling logic

- The population is composed of stakeholder groups related to the Camino de Santiago.
- The thesis names pilgrims, travellers, hostel managers, Camino associations, and representatives of municipalities, regions, and churches.
- The sample was selected by criterion rather than statistical representativeness.
- The thesis reports 24 interviews in total.

This is important for the chapter because it confirms that the original fieldwork was stakeholder-focused, exploratory, and non-inferential.

## Interview architecture

- The thesis used two interview instruments.
- The interviews were deployed in three languages: Spanish, English, and French.
- Some were written, while others were oral and virtual.
- The first interview contained 14 questions focused on sustainability on the Camino, tourism development, degrowth, and adaptive capacity.
- The second interview contained 12 questions focused on protection measures, security controls, COVID-19 contagion, and sustainable tourism in that context.

## Analytical categories directly named in the thesis

The thesis explicitly identifies these variables or categories for analysis:

1. sustainable tourism
2. degrowth
3. resilience
4. adaptive capacity
5. local development
6. economic impacts
7. social impacts
8. environmental impacts

These categories are consistent with the thesis table of contents, where the results are organized around sustainable tourism, local development and Agenda 21, commons and social capital, mass pilgrimage and degrowth, and resilience in pandemic times.

## What is transferable to the chapter

The thesis provides a replicable qualitative logic rather than a ready-made empirical extension to Santander. The transferable elements are:

1. Define a route-based sustainability problem in a specific territorial context.
2. Delimit the route and identify relevant stakeholder groups.
3. Build a documentary baseline before any field phase.
4. Select analytical categories tied to sustainability, development, and resilience.
5. Design interviews or other qualitative instruments around those categories.
6. Compare positive and negative economic, social, and environmental impacts.
7. Translate findings into governance and management recommendations.

## Adaptation rule for the present chapter

The current chapter must not reuse the thesis as if it were new fieldwork in Santander. What can be adapted responsibly is:

- the problem-framing logic,
- the stakeholder-oriented structure,
- the category system,
- the stepwise progression from documentary review to qualitative inquiry,
- and the practice-oriented emphasis on recommendations.

What cannot be transferred without new fieldwork is:

- the interview evidence itself,
- the sample,
- the pandemic-specific responses,
- or any empirical result for the Camino de Lengerke.
